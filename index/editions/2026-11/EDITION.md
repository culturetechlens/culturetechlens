## The question

Which moments does the record keep returning to — and which ones is the public
still discovering? The ten most resonant moments in the Black Chicago record,
ranked on the combined signal of public attention and CTL's own verified research.

Rankings describe the data. They do not crown winners.

## The CTL 10 — Moments, November 2026

**1. Emmett Till funeral at Roberts Temple (1955) — 68.5**
The set's highest public attention by far (103,248 lookups) meets solid CTL
documentation: 8 verified claims across six research packages.

**2. Chicago Freedom Movement: MLK in Chicago (1966) — 52.2**

**7. Harold Washington elected Chicago's first Black mayor (1983) — 26.4**

**8. The Warehouse opens (1977) — 23.7**

## How it was scored

- **Public attention (40%):** trailing-30-day Wikipedia pageviews per subject's closest English article, log-normalized across the set. Each article was verified to exist; where no standalone article exists, the closest article is used (Emmett Till funeral → Emmett Till; Bud Billiken Parade → Bud Billiken Parade and Picnic).
- **Evidence depth (40%):** from the CTL flagship timeline corpus — the verified timeline entries of the 14 deep-research packages. Same formula family as Edition 2026-10 (verified in the October evidence table): 2.0 × verified claims + 1.0 × provisional claims + 1.5 × verified relationships + 5.0 full-event-treatment bonus (a dedicated event/campaign/movement entity in the corpus — the moments-domain analogue of the Object Biography bonus). Built by an auditable script (`build-evidence-2026-11.py`); evidence table `edition-2026-11-evidence.json`. Normalized across the set.
- **Momentum (20%):** pageview trend, trailing 30 days vs. prior 30 days; 50 = flat. This is the first Moments edition, so there are no cross-edition trend arrows yet.
- Computed by `ctl-index.py`; no hand-tuned scores. Full per-signal data: `index-scores.csv`.

## Limitations

- Wikipedia pageviews skew toward subjects with English Wikipedia articles and internet-era attention.
- CTL's corpus reflects what CTL has researched so far; under-documented subjects score lower on evidence depth by construction. This edition surfaces two such gaps openly (Soul Train, the 1919 Race Riot).
- Moment-to-article mapping is interpretive: the Till funeral is scored on the Emmett Till article's attention, not a funeral-specific article. This inflates attention for the funeral relative to a narrower article; the mapping is disclosed so the number stays honest.
- The Index reflects resonance in the record, not community sentiment on the ground.
- Ranks move month to month; movement is signal, not verdict. The Bud Billiken Parade's momentum drop is seasonal (post-August parade), not a decline in cultural standing.
