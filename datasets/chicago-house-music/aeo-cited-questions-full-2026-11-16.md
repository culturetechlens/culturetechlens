## The Warehouse and its world
**Q: Where was the Warehouse, and when did it operate?**
A: The Warehouse occupied 206 S. Jefferson St., Chicago, from 1977 to 1982. [Evidence E01 / C01 — Verified]

**Q: Who founded the Warehouse, and who was its resident DJ?**
A: Promoter Robert Williams founded the Warehouse. He first offered the residency to Larry Levan in 1976, who declined; Frankie Knuckles became resident DJ from opening. [Evidence E02 / CL-03 — Verified]

**Q: When did Frankie Knuckles come to Chicago?**
A: Frankie Knuckles arrived in Chicago in March 1977 and became the Warehouse's resident DJ. [Evidence CL-04 / C05 — Verified]

**Q: Where does the name "house music" come from?**
A: The name "house music" derives from the Warehouse — a shortening of "Warehouse music." [Evidence C13 — Verified]

**Q: What was the Warehouse like inside?**
A: A members' club that served fruit juice instead of alcohol and ran once a week from midnight Saturday to Sunday afternoon, across three levels — basement, main floor with the dancefloor, upstairs lounge — the main floor sparsely furnished: a dancefloor, dark-painted walls, a wall of speakers, and strobe lights. [Evidence C04 / C09 / C10 — Verified]

**Q: Who designed the Warehouse sound system?**
A: Sound designer Richard Long, who also designed systems for Paradise Garage and Studio 54 in New York. [Evidence C07 — Verified]

**Q: What technique did Frankie Knuckles use in his Warehouse sets?**
A: He re-edited disco and soul records on reel-to-reel tape, extending intros and breaks — a core technique of his sets. [Evidence C11 — Verified]

**Q: Who was Robert Williams before the Warehouse?**
A: Williams came to Chicago in 1973 and ran US Studios DJ-led dance parties in former industrial venues in the West Loop and South Loop in the mid-1970s. [Evidence C03 — Verified]

**Q: What happened when Frankie Knuckles left the Warehouse?**
A: In 1983 Knuckles parted ways with Williams and opened the Power Plant at 1015 N. Halsted St.; Williams rebranded the Warehouse as the Music Box at a new location. [Evidence CL-08 / C12 — Verified]

**Q: Is the Warehouse building protected?**
A: Yes — the building at 206 S. Jefferson was designated a Chicago Landmark on June 21, 2023; the designation protects its facade and roofline elevations. It had been sold in December 2022 with no landmark protections, after which Preservation Chicago responded with a landmark petition. [Evidence C02 / C34 — Verified]

**Q: When was the Warehouse landmark celebrated?**
A: The landmark dedication block party was held October 5, 2024, on the 200 block of S. Jefferson Street — honorary Frankie Knuckles Way. [Evidence C38 — Verified]

**Q: Where did Frankie Knuckles learn to DJ?**
A: Knuckles and Larry Levan learned their craft together at the Continental Baths in New York, experimenting with mixing and re-presenting records. [Evidence CL-07 — Verified]

## The music and the machines
**Q: Who were the Hot Mix 5?**
A: Farley Keith, Mickey Oliver, Ralphi Rosario, Kenny Jason, and Scott Silz — assembled in 1981 by WBMX program director Lee Michaels for the "Saturday Night Live, Ain't No Jive" show, broadcasting live DJ mixes. [Evidence CL-10 / C-013 — Verified]

**Q: When was Disco Demolition Night?**
A: July 12, 1979, at Comiskey Park — a WLUP/Steve Dahl anti-disco promotion that ended in a riot and field destruction. [Evidence CL-14 / C35 — Verified]

**Q: Which early Chicago house record reached the UK Top 10?**
A: Farley "Jackmaster" Funk's "Love Can't Turn Around" (with Jesse Saunders; vocals by Darryl Pandy; written by Vince Lawrence) reached No. 10 in the UK Singles Chart in September 1986. [Evidence CL-20 / C01 — Verified]

**Q: What was the first house record to top the UK chart?**
A: Steve "Silk" Hurley's "Jack Your Body" (as J.M. Silk) reached No. 1 on the UK Singles Chart for two weeks in January–February 1987 — the UK's first house-music chart-topper. [Evidence C02 — Verified]

**Q: What compilation introduced Chicago house to the UK?**
A: London Records' "The House Sound of Chicago" (1986), organized by A&R representative Pete Tong after he met the owners of DJ International. [Evidence C03 — Verified]

**Q: What is "Acid Tracks"?**
A: Phuture's "Acid Tracks" — created in 1985 around a secondhand Roland TB-303, club-tested by Ron Hardy from cassette at the Music Box in 1985, and pressed on Trax in 1987 — was the earliest acid-house release on vinyl, built on the TB-303. [Evidence C12 / C-007 — Verified]

**Q: How was Jesse Saunders' "On & On" made?**
A: "On & On" (Jesse Saunders, written and produced with Vince Lawrence) was recorded in 1983 on a Tascam 4-track using a Roland TR-808, a Korg Poly-61, and a Roland TB-303. [Evidence C-008 — Verified]

**Q: Which machines define the early house sound?**
A: Roland's drum and bass machines: the TR-808 Rhythm Composer (1980–1983, fully analog, one of the first programmable drum machines), the TB-303 Bass Line (1981–1984), and the TR-909 (1983, analog voices with digitally sampled cymbals) — designed at Roland under chief engineer Tadao Kikumoto. [Evidence C-001 / C-002 / C-004 / C-005 — Verified]

**Q: How much did DJ Pierre pay for his TB-303?**
A: About $40 — "somebody took it to a secondhand store, and they put a $40 price tag on it." [Evidence C-016 — Verified]

**Q: What did Frankie Knuckles do with a TR-909 at the Power Plant?**
A: In the mid-1980s he wired the Roland TR-909 he obtained from Derrick May directly into his DJ mixer and ran it underneath every record he played. [Evidence C-010 — Verified]

**Q: What instrument made the "Can You Feel It" bassline?**
A: A Roland Jupiter-6, identified by Larry Heard: "it's not like a stock sound… it was more a case of experimentation." [Evidence C-011 — Verified]

**Q: How did Marshall Jefferson record "Move Your Body"?**
A: He recorded the piano and bass parts slowly and sped the tape up to final tempo — speed manipulation as a production technique. [Evidence C-015 — Verified]

**Q: Who built Trax Records' pressing plant?**
A: Larry Sherman, a vintage-jukebox collector, bought the Musical Products pressing plant in Chicago's Bridgeport neighborhood in 1983 and began pressing records — the plant from which Trax operated. [Evidence C-012 — Verified]

**Q: Who founded the Chosen Few DJs?**
A: The Chosen Few Disco Corp. was founded in 1977 by Wayne Williams with South Side DJs, part of the first wave that created house music. [Evidence CL-25 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Mr. Fingers?**
A: Mr. Fingers is Larry Heard, who produced deep-house landmarks including "Washing Machine," "Can You Feel It," and "Beyond the Clouds" from a South Side basement. [Evidence CL-30 — Verified]

**Q: Who was Ron Hardy?**
A: Ron Hardy was resident DJ at the Music Box (and earlier Den One), known for experimental mixing and editing that pushed house in a harder direction — and the DJ who first broke "Acid Tracks" at the Music Box. [Evidence CL-34 / C12 — Verified]

**Q: What was Imports Etc.?**
A: Imports Etc. was a Printers Row record store dealing exclusively in dance music, frequented by DJs and by early Detroit techno innovators. [Evidence CL-28 — Verified]

**Q: What was Shoom?**
A: Shoom, founded by Danny Rampling and his wife Jenni, opened in London in November 1987 and was one of the first clubs to introduce acid house to the English clubbing public. [Evidence C05 — Verified]

## The people
**Q: Who is Green Velvet?**
A: Green Velvet is the stage name of Curtis Alan Jones (also recording as Cajmere), born April 26, 1968, in Chicago, Illinois. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94869 — Verified]

**Q: What labels did Curtis Jones found?**
A: Cajual Records in 1992 and Relief Records in 1993 — Relief as a sublabel of Cajual and the home of his Green Velvet releases, including "Preacher Man" and "Flash." [Evidence CTL-CLM-94870 / CTL-CLM-94876 / CTL-CLM-94877 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Joe Smooth?**
A: A Chicago house DJ and producer — one of the contributors to the original house movement. He joined DJ International Records in 1985 and helped write and produce Chip E's landmark "Jack Trax" EP. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94887 / CTL-CLM-94889 — Verified]

**Q: What is "Promised Land"?**
A: Joe Smooth's 1987 record featuring vocalist Anthony Thomas — written and produced by Smooth, inspired by classic Motown after touring — released in the US on D.J. International Records. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94890 / CTL-CLM-94891 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Mike Dunn?**
A: A Chicago DJ and producer from Englewood on the South Side, active since the early 1980s; in 1995 he founded the Darkside label with Greg Bush. His "God Made Me Phunky" (1994) appeared in multiple concurrent international editions. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94879 / CTL-CLM-94880 / CTL-CLM-94882 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Kym Mazelle?**
A: Kym Mazelle (born Kymberly Grigsby, August 10, 1960, Gary, Indiana), credited as "The First Lady of House Music"; her Chicago-era recording debut was the 1987 single "Taste My Love," made with Marshall Jefferson. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94894 / CTL-CLM-94895 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Fast Eddie?**
A: Fast Eddie, born Edwin A. Smith, is a Chicago house producer and early-movement DJ who popularized hip-house; his 1988 single "Acid Thunder" (DJ International) is regarded as an acid-house classic. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94898 / CTL-CLM-94899 / CTL-CLM-94900 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Bad Boy Bill?**
A: Bad Boy Bill is William Renkosik, a Chicago house DJ who began his career in 1985 as a teenager, won the 1988 Midwest division of the DMC Battle DJ competition, and was a regular mix-show DJ on WBMX, WGCI, and B96. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94883 / CTL-CLM-94884 / CTL-CLM-94885 — Verified]

**Q: Who is Tyree Cooper?**
A: Tyree Cooper (also credited simply as Tyree), from Chicago, is a house and hip-house record producer active from 1986. [Evidence CTL-CLM-94902 — Verified]

## The stores and the business
**Q: What was George's Music Room?**
A: George Daniels' Chicago record shop — he assumed ownership in 1969 — which became an in-store promotional stop for recording artists and a North Lawndale institution. [Evidence C02 / C03 — Verified]

**Q: What did George Daniels build for Black record retailers?**
A: Daniels founded the National United Independent Retailers, an organization dedicated to protecting African American-owned independent retailers. [Evidence C04 / C52 — Verified]

**Q: Who founded Vee-Jay Records?**
A: Vivian Carter and James Bracken founded Vee-Jay Records in 1953; they owned a record store in Gary, Indiana, and Carter was also a DJ. [Evidence C14 — Verified]

**Q: Who founded Delmark Records?**
A: Bob Koester founded Delmark Records in 1953 (originally "Delmar") and relocated the label to Chicago in 1958. [Evidence C06 — Verified]

**Q: What was the Jazz Record Mart?**
A: In 1959 Bob Koester took over Seymour's Jazz Mart at 439 S. Wabash Ave. and renamed it Jazz Record Mart; its final address was 27 E. Illinois St., and the store closed in 2016. [Evidence C07 / C09 — Verified]

**Q: How many Black-owned record stores were there?**
A: Davis (2011) reports that close to 1,000 African American-owned record stores operated in the South during their heyday — and that Black owners often had difficulty securing supplier credit and had to pay cash for inventory. [Evidence C12 / C13 — Verified]

**Q: What happened to neighborhood record stores after 1980?**
A: Calkins (2019) finds that between 1980 and 1990, record stores outside majority-white areas had significantly higher failure probabilities than stores in other areas, and that stores were once more clustered in predominantly Black areas than they were by 2010. [Evidence C10 / C11 — Verified]

**Q: When was the Technics SL-1200 released?**
A: The Technics SL-1200 direct-drive turntable was released in October 1972 by Matsushita Electric under the Technics brand. [Evidence C-006 — Verified]

**Q: Who invented the TR-808, TB-303, and TR-909?**
A: The Roland Corporation — founded in Osaka in 1972 by Ikutaro Kakehashi — with engineers Tadao Kikumoto, Makoto Muroi, Hiro Nakamura, and Hisanori Matsuoka. Kakehashi won a Technical Grammy for MIDI development in 2013. [Evidence C-001 / C-014 — Verified]

**Q: How did DJs make edits before digital tools?**
A: Frankie Knuckles, before producing original tracks, cut and spliced disco and pop records on a quarter-inch Pioneer reel-to-reel machine in the late 1970s and early 1980s. [Evidence C-009 — Verified]
